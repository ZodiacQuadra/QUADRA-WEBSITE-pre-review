HYBRID CLOUD & DIGITAL WORK · RESILIENCE PLATFORM OPTIONS

Turn Protected Data into a Tested Recovery Plan

A backup platform must support the recovery path your business actually needs. Quadra evaluates Veeam for virtualised, hybrid, cloud and SaaS environments, connecting workload protection and recovery orchestration with the data-risk, privacy and governance requirements of the engagement.

Discuss a Veeam recovery roadmap
01

Start with protection and recovery requirements

Evaluate Veeam Data Platform against supported workloads, restore granularity, deployment choices and operational responsibilities. Compare cloud, Kubernetes, SaaS, on-premises and identity needs individually. Portability can broaden recovery options, but formats, application dependencies, licences and destination capacity still matter.

  • Use Veeam Data Platform as the starting point for the backup and recovery design.
  • For broader data-security or AI-governance needs, scope a separate evaluation of relationship visibility, access controls and integration with existing tools.
  • Document the selected edition, supported workloads, connectors and recovery destinations in the solution scope.
02

Find and prioritise data exposure

Assess data security posture management requirements across supported cloud, SaaS and on-premises data. Sensitive-data discovery and classification should lead to a prioritised remediation plan with clear control owners.

  • Identify public links, excessive permissions, over-sharing and combinations of exposure that increase business risk.
  • Prioritise findings by data sensitivity, business impact and the practical remediation path.
  • Use threat-detection and malware-scanning evidence to inform investigation, recovery-point selection and restored-service validation.
03

Control how people and AI use protected information

For organisations extending their resilience programme into AI governance, a separately scoped assessment can define the inventory, access controls and recovery evidence needed. Work with the established governance team to choose appropriate tools and integrations before implementation.

  • Define visibility requirements for sanctioned and previously unknown agents, copilots and models, including the telemetry and connectors needed.
  • Establish how permission drift will be identified and how identity and data owners make least-privilege decisions.
  • Evaluate data-layer policy enforcement for the required applications, users and machine identities.
  • Map recovery from unwanted AI actions, including application state, external transactions and downstream effects that need separate remediation.

Enterprise AI Engineering remains the route for AI lifecycle and governance. Coordinate Microsoft controls with Microsoft Security and non-Microsoft cyber controls with Enterprise Cybersecurity.

04

Make privacy requirements part of the data design

Map personal and sensitive data to the systems, users and locations involved. Assess policies by data type, role and jurisdiction with the customer’s privacy and legal owners; residency is a design requirement, not a property inferred from a platform name.

  • Document data location, transfers, retention and restore destinations.
  • Evaluate jurisdiction-aware policy support against the actual licence and integrations.
  • Review redundant, obsolete and trivial data for minimisation, preserving required retention and legal holds before authorising deletion.
05

Generate evidence that supports—not replaces—assurance

Assess automated checks, reports and control mappings against the organisation’s applicable obligations. Requirements may include GDPR, HIPAA, DORA and the EU AI Act, alongside frameworks such as NIST and the AI RMF. A scoped Quadra advisory workstream can review mapping to India’s DPDP Act and relevant RBI, SEBI or IRDAI expectations. The customer’s advisers determine applicability and legal interpretation; platform evidence supports their compliance review.

  • Map each required control to the evidence available in the selected product and release.
  • Evaluate hardened immutability and four-eyes approval for supported changes, with separation of duties and emergency procedures.
  • Assess Veeam Recovery Orchestrator for supported vSphere, Hyper-V and Azure recovery targets, subject to edition and release eligibility. Plan isolated test environments, capacity and production safeguards.
  • Retain test results, approvals, exceptions and remediation evidence for the agreed review process.
06

Recover at the granularity the incident requires

Choose recovery methods according to the damaged service, available restore points and validated application dependencies. A granular restore may be appropriate for an object or configuration problem; an infrastructure or identity compromise may require a wider recovery sequence.

  • Configure immutable protection where supported and evaluate pre-restore malware scanning with the security responders.
  • Test precise object- or data-level restores as well as the full service-recovery path.
  • Veeam Data Platform v13.1 introduces guided Active Directory forest recovery. Confirm the selected edition, prerequisites, access and supported automation; test the directory recovery sequence rather than treating every edition or release as equivalent.
  • Where appropriate, assess Coveware by Veeam for separately scoped cyber-extortion incident-response support, eligibility and escalation.

Test restoration times against the agreed recovery objectives, using representative data volumes, infrastructure, connectivity and application-validation steps.

07

Build the evidence before selecting the scope

A scoped engagement can produce the workload and support matrix, protection policy, dependency-led recovery plan, identity-recovery prerequisites, test evidence and operating runbooks. Compare licensing, storage, cloud use, egress, test capacity, migration and support costs. Establish achieved RTO and RPO results through representative exercises.

  • Bring your Veeam estate or target workloads, retention requirements and the incidents or tests that exposed recovery gaps.
  • Define customer-operated, co-managed or managed responsibilities, service hours, approvals and escalation in the commercial scope.
  • Expand into data security, privacy or AI-related features only when their support and cross-practice ownership have been established.
