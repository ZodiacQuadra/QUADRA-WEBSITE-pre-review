HYBRID CLOUD & DIGITAL WORK · RESILIENCE PLATFORM OPTIONS

SaaS-Led Resilience for a Distributed Enterprise

Managing backup infrastructure can distract teams from the harder question: can the business recover? Quadra evaluates Druva for SaaS-led protection across supported cloud, data-centre, endpoint, SaaS and identity workloads, with recovery, preservation and customer responsibilities designed into the service.

Discuss a Druva resilience design
01

Reduce infrastructure administration—not accountability

Druva Resilience Cloud is a SaaS-led technology candidate for the resilience design. A provider-managed backup service changes infrastructure and lifecycle responsibilities; the customer still needs appropriate access, supported connectors or agents, workload onboarding, network capacity, policies and a tested restore path.

  • Confirm supported workloads, tenant configuration, licensing, available regions and permitted recovery destinations.
  • Evaluate Dru MetaGraph and related intelligence capabilities against current availability and the data or relationships they actually cover.
  • Compare provider-managed infrastructure with customer-operated alternatives using whole-life costs and recovery requirements.

Recover, govern, defend and enable AI—within a tested scope

AI services can change data and application state faster than traditional recovery procedures anticipate. Establish the objects, content and permissions available for backup and restoration before claiming protection of an AI workspace.

01

Recover

Evaluate recovery from unwanted autonomous actions at the supported data or configuration level. Validate dependencies and downstream consequences; not all actions can be reversed.

02

Govern

Evaluate supported Microsoft 365 Copilot prompts, responses and generated files. For Claude, scope protection to Claude Code and Cowork data on managed Windows and macOS endpoints; Claude.ai cloud chat history is outside this protection scope. Confirm supported files, retention and restoration granularity.

03

Defend

Review backup administration, access separation, anomaly signals and response procedures against AI-enabled attacks as part of the wider security programme.

04

Enable

Evaluate DruAI and Druva MCP for connecting approved AI assistants to backup and recovery intelligence. Define least-privilege access, allowed actions, human approvals and audit records.

Scope the supported AI content, integrations and recovery actions for the selected service. Coordinate AI lifecycle and restart decisions with Enterprise AI Engineering.

03

Protect distributed business data

Evaluate Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Salesforce, VMware, endpoint and cloud-workload protection against the supported service and object matrix. SaaS-led scaling can reduce backup-infrastructure administration; onboarding time and recovery performance still depend on the workload, connectivity and configuration.

  • Protect agreed business-critical laptop and desktop data, including the recovery and retention needs of departed employees.
  • Select regions and data flows according to the chosen workload and service availability, residency obligations and recovery destinations. Region support varies by workload; a portfolio-wide count does not establish availability for a particular application.
  • Model licence, retained capacity, data transfer, recovery, implementation and operational costs against the existing environment. Validate savings rather than applying a vendor percentage.
04

Recover the identity foundation in the right order

Evaluate Druva Identity Resilience for Active Directory, Microsoft Entra ID and Okta protection, including supported forest-level recovery. The offering uses Enterprise per-user licensing; confirm entitlements and supported objects for each provider. Map directory and authentication dependencies to the business recovery plan, including privileged access and emergency credentials.

  • Active Directory: assess granular object restore and guided forest-level recovery, with domain-controller availability and the supported recovery procedure tested before commitment.
  • Microsoft Entra ID: confirm which users, groups, roles, memberships and relationships can be restored and what dependencies or exclusions apply.
  • Okta: validate the supported identity configurations, objects and restoration sequence.
  • Security: review encryption in transit and at rest, key-management responsibilities, administrative access and recovery prerequisites for the selected service.

Backup and recovery of a Microsoft identity workload does not move Microsoft Security ownership into HCDW. The directory owner and Microsoft Security specialists remain part of the recovery and control decisions.

05

Use recovery evidence to narrow uncertainty

Establish what incident responders can learn from logs, changed-data visibility, proactive detection and threat-hunting tools. Feed that evidence into recovery-point selection and an authorised, isolated validation process.

  • Configure isolation and immutability with the appropriate retention and administrative boundaries.
  • Evaluate anomaly detection and threat hunting across supported backups for indicators of compromise.
  • Select a candidate last-known-good snapshot using available evidence, then validate restored data and services before reconnecting.
  • Define how investigation, containment and recovery handoffs work with the security responders.

Document the evidence behind the selected recovery point and complete the agreed security and business acceptance checks before service restart.

06

Make preservation and eDiscovery defensible

Assess whether existing backups can support authorised collection, preservation and investigation for the required endpoint and SaaS data. The legal owner defines the matter, custodians, preservation obligations and release authority.

  • Legal hold: evaluate preservation across supported endpoints and SaaS sources, including exclusions from ordinary deletion and the release workflow.
  • Federated search: validate files, versions, deleted items and departed-employee data against the retained and indexed content.
  • Evidence integrity: review audit trails, chain-of-custody records and alignment with the organisation’s Electronic Discovery Reference Model (EDRM) process.
  • Compliance monitoring: assess relevant GDPR, HIPAA and PCI templates and supported eDiscovery integrations such as Exterro.

A backup and an audit log are inputs to a defensible process, not automatic proof of admissibility or compliance. Collection scope, integrity checks, access and legal review still matter.

07

Define the service you will actually operate

A scoped engagement can produce the workload and object matrix, residency and data-flow design, retention and hold policies, identity-recovery sequence, restore-test evidence and operating runbooks. Confirm the selected features and integrations with the OEM before committing the design.

  • Compare achieved recovery timings and data currency with the agreed RTO and RPO objectives.
  • Agree how Druva’s service, Quadra’s managed delivery and the customer’s administrators share responsibilities, service hours and escalation.
  • Bring your distributed workforce, SaaS and identity priorities to scope a pilot or assessment with defined outputs and commercial terms.
