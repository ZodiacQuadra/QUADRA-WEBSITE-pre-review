HYBRID CLOUD & DIGITAL WORK · RESILIENCE PLATFORM OPTIONS

Unified Resilience at Enterprise Scale

Complex estates can accumulate separate backup policies, recovery tools and ownership gaps. Quadra helps evaluate and implement a Commvault-based design that connects protection, identity dependencies and recovery operations across agreed hybrid, cloud and SaaS workloads—starting with the services the business needs to restore.

Discuss a Commvault resilience design
01

Choose the operating model as carefully as the platform

Commvault is an OEM technology option for estates seeking a coordinated protection and recovery approach. Compare software, SaaS and appliance delivery, skills, lifecycle responsibilities and workload coverage. For cloud-native AWS requirements, evaluate the role of Clumio by Commvault within the design.

  • Assess the Commvault Cloud Unity platform release and its Resilience Ops approach against your protection and recovery priorities.
  • Agree the release, licence, region, integrations and supported recovery paths for the selected deployment.
  • Retain specialist tools where workload or control requirements justify them, and consolidate where replacement coverage is proven.
02

Data protection across the agreed workload estate

Build a protection matrix and policy framework for the required workloads, then test representative restores. Coverage is established at workload and object level—not inferred from a broad platform label.

  • Virtual machines, databases and file systems across supported on-premises environments.
  • Cloud-native applications and data on AWS, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud, subject to the relevant service and connector support.
  • SaaS applications such as Microsoft 365, Google Workspace and Salesforce; DevOps repositories and AI data where supported.
  • Isolated and immutable copies with retention, administrator separation and recovery-access requirements explicitly configured.
03

Recover the application context as well as its data

Application configurations and cloud infrastructure may be needed alongside the protected data. Define how incident findings inform recovery-point selection, where restoration occurs and who approves reconnection.

  • Evaluate Threat Scan and clean-point identification to investigate compromised data and select a candidate recovery point.
  • Assess Synthetic Recovery for supported selective recovery scenarios; establish what can be excluded, reconstructed or restored.
  • Evaluate an on-demand Commvault Cleanroom for isolated recovery testing, investigation and validation, with capacity, connectivity and evidence access defined.
  • Test supported cross-cloud, cross-hypervisor or cleanroom recovery paths and the route back to production.

Combine scanning and isolation with application checks and security approval before reconnecting recovered services.

04

Protect the systems that control access

Map Tier 0 identity dependencies, the most privileged assets and the people or services that control them. Scope supported Active Directory and Microsoft Entra ID recovery around the objects, permissions and dependencies needed to restore business access. The design specifies the recovery method for each identity provider and workload.

  • Identify the identity, permission and access-change evidence needed to investigate an incident and choose a recovery point.
  • Map privileged exposure and emergency access with the directory and security owners.
  • For organisations exploring broader identity resilience, scope a separate evaluation of cross-provider auditing, guided remediation and configuration rollback.
  • Define object-level and wider directory-recovery requirements separately, then test the selected supported paths and prerequisites.

This is an identity-recovery workstream. Microsoft-specific security architecture and controls remain with Microsoft Security; directory owners approve restoration and access changes.

05

Know sensitive data—and who or what can reach it

Evaluate discovery and classification of sensitive and personal information across supported data stores and applications. Connect findings to protection policy and access decisions for people and AI. Where broader access governance is needed, assess the required integrations as a separate workstream with the responsible data and security owners.

  • Assess AI-assisted classification and policy recommendations against representative customer data.
  • Define the scope of data and AI access monitoring and enforcement, including the systems where controls actually apply.
  • Evaluate identity, access and data anomaly signals, together with supported third-party threat-intelligence integrations.

Coordinate non-Microsoft data-security controls with Enterprise Cybersecurity, Microsoft controls with Microsoft Security and AI lifecycle decisions with Enterprise AI Engineering.

06

Resilience without an untested cost assumption

Compare the cost and operating effort of consolidation with the existing estate. Include licensing, retained data, infrastructure, cloud consumption, egress, recovery environments, support and migration. Native snapshot management and cloud cost visibility can inform the assessment where supported.

  • Evaluate automation and the Arlie assistant for configuration, troubleshooting and recovery guidance, with appropriate access and approval gates.
  • Measure operator effort and recovery-test outcomes in a scoped pilot rather than carrying vendor TCO comparisons into the business case as a promise.
  • Keep tool retirement and any claimed savings conditional on validated replacement coverage.
07

Keep AI data recoverable before putting it to further use

Scope protection for data lakes, pipelines, model artefacts and the configuration or infrastructure needed to recreate an AI service. Immutability and isolation protect retained copies; application and AI owners still need to validate data integrity, permissions and model behaviour after restoration.

  • Assess poisoning and machine-identity attack scenarios with the security and AI teams; backup alone does not detect or resolve every AI threat.
  • If reuse of protected data for analytics or AI is a priority, scope a separate evaluation of classification, export formats and the receiving data platform.
  • Apply purpose, consent, retention and access checks before reusing backup data; a protected copy is not automatic permission to train or analyse.
  • Metallic AI is Commvault Cloud’s AI and automation layer; Arlie is its AI assistant. Evaluate supported detection, recovery-orchestration and guidance workflows with appropriate permissions, approvals and feature-availability checks.
08

What a scoped engagement produces

Agree a workload and dependency matrix, platform and deployment comparison, policy design, recovery sequence, pilot and test record, operating runbooks and a commercial model. Test the selected recovery paths against service-specific RTO and RPO objectives. Managed delivery is defined through agreed coverage, service hours, customer approvals and escalation—not by the breadth of the OEM portfolio.

  • Bring current Commvault entitlements or the replacement scope, the critical-service list and recent recovery findings.
  • Use the results to decide what to consolidate, what to retain and which recovery gaps to address first.
