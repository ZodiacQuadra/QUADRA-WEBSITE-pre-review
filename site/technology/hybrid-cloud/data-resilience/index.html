HYBRID CLOUD & DIGITAL WORK · CAPABILITY

Recover the Business—not Just the Backup

Ransomware, accidental change and service failure create different recovery problems. Quadra connects data protection, identity recovery and cyber-recovery preparation to the business services you need to restore. The result is an agreed recovery design, a tested sequence and evidence of what works—and what still needs attention.

Scope a resilience readiness assessmentExplore this area
01

Define the recovery outcome before choosing the tool

Start with business impact and dependencies across on-premises, cloud, SaaS, endpoints and AI-enabled workflows. Set an RTO for the time to restore a service and an RPO for acceptable data loss. These are design and test objectives, not guarantees inherited from a product.

  • Map applications, data, identities, network services, keys and external dependencies to business owners.
  • Distinguish availability, replication, backup, disaster recovery and cyber recovery; each addresses different failure modes.
  • Record retention, legal hold, residency, access and restoration requirements by workload.
  • Identify who declares an incident, approves containment, selects a recovery point and accepts the restored service.
02

Protect copies. Validate the path back.

Design isolation, immutability and access separation around the threat model and supported platform behaviour. A retained copy is useful only if credentials, keys, catalogues and recovery capacity are also available.

  • Protect agreed virtual machines, databases, files, cloud-native workloads, SaaS applications, repositories and endpoints.
  • Define backup administration, retention locks, privileged access and approval controls; distinguish logical isolation from a physically disconnected copy.
  • Assess malware scanning, anomaly signals, threat hunting and recovery-point selection as inputs to validation—not proof that no compromise remains.
  • Use isolated recovery environments where appropriate to test applications, configuration and data before reconnecting to production.
  • Plan cross-environment recovery and failback where supported, accounting for format, bandwidth, egress, capacity and licensing constraints.
03

Identity, data security and AI resilience belong in the same plan

Identity services control who can administer the recovery and who can use the restored application. Map Active Directory, Microsoft Entra ID and Okta dependencies, privileged accounts, machine identities and emergency access. Establish a dependency-led recovery sequence; directory recovery may need to precede application restart, but the order must reflect the actual environment.

  • Evaluate object, configuration, relationship and forest-level recovery separately; providers and products do not offer identical restore granularity.
  • Coordinate sensitive-data discovery, classification, access review and eDiscovery with the teams responsible for security, privacy and legal decisions.
  • For AI workloads, identify protected datasets, data lakes, pipelines, model artefacts, configuration and supported workspace content.
  • Before an AI service resumes, validate data provenance and integrity, permissions, connectors and representative outputs with the AI owner. A successful restore does not by itself make a model trustworthy.
  • Treat recovery from unwanted agent actions as a scoped application and data problem; not every external transaction can be reversed by restoring a backup.

A practical resilience blueprint

01

Assess

Create a business-service and workload inventory, review existing test evidence and identify protection, dependency and operating gaps.

02

Architect & Engineer

Define recovery tiers, policies, copy isolation, access and the target recovery environment. Produce a platform comparison, cost assumptions and a sequenced implementation plan.

03

Validate & Prepare

Run authorised restore and recovery exercises. Capture achieved timings, data currency, application checks, exceptions and the owners of corrective actions.

04

Operate & Improve

Monitor agreed protection services, review changes and run the scheduled test programme. Maintain runbooks, escalation paths and an improvement backlog.

06

One recovery plan, explicit decision rights

HCDW coordinates the infrastructure and data-recovery workstream. Microsoft Security retains Microsoft-specific control expertise; Enterprise Cybersecurity covers non-Microsoft controls and the related incident workstream; Enterprise AI Engineering supports AI lifecycle, evaluation and governance. Agree the lead and decision rights for each engagement rather than assuming a product name determines ownership.

  • Security responders establish containment requirements and contribute evidence for recovery-point selection.
  • Identity owners authorise directory restoration, credential changes and privileged access.
  • Application and business owners validate restored services; AI owners validate the AI workflow before restart.
  • Legal and privacy owners set preservation and regulatory requirements. Reporting supports their review; it does not confer compliance.
07

Make the limits visible before an incident

A recovery exercise demonstrates the tested scenario at that time, not every possible incident. Record exclusions, data volumes, network conditions, third-party dependencies and any unsupported objects. The operating agreement specifies service hours, response commitments, retention, test cadence and customer approvals.

  • Use measured test results to compare achieved recovery with the agreed RTO and RPO.
  • Keep audit logs, approvals and test records with an agreed evidence-retention policy.
  • Review unresolved risk, recovery cost and the next improvement actions with business owners.
08

Scope a resilience readiness assessment

Bring the critical-service list, backup inventory, identity dependencies and your latest recovery-test findings. Agree a bounded assessment, access requirements, deliverables and commercial terms. The next step may be a targeted restore test, a platform comparison or a phased recovery improvement programme—not necessarily a platform replacement.
