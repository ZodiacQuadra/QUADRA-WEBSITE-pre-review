HYBRID CLOUD & DIGITAL WORK · PRACTICE

Resilient Hybrid Cloud for the AI-Ready Enterprise

Infrastructure choices that fit the workload. Workspaces that support people. Recovery that is designed and tested.

A critical application is only useful if people can reach it, its data can be trusted and the business can recover when something goes wrong. Quadra connects private and hybrid cloud infrastructure, digital workspaces and data and identity recovery in one architecture shaped around your workloads, workforce and business priorities.

Talk to a Hybrid Cloud ArchitectExplore this area
01

Cloud. AI. Security. One Architect.

Cloud choice is a workload and economics decision, not a destination mandate. AI depends on recoverable data, trustworthy identities and controlled access to business systems. Security must support both prevention and recovery, while the workspace makes those foundations usable by employees. One Architect keeps business intent, architectural context and responsibility connected through specialist hand-offs, from design into operation.

02

A successful backup is not a recovered business

Lost access, corrupted data or an unavailable application can interrupt customer service and employee work even when backup jobs report success. AI adds dependencies on data pipelines, models, connectors and machine identities; restoring files alone does not establish that an AI-enabled workflow is safe to resume.

  • Define which business services must return first, what data loss is tolerable and which people authorise recovery.
  • Understand the identity, network, application and data dependencies behind each service.
  • Test restoration and business usability, then record the gaps instead of assuming a backup or an availability feature proves resilience.

Four integrated pillars. One recovery-aware design.

Infrastructure, workspace and resilience remain the three capability routes. Identity, data security and AI resilience run across them and are developed within the resilience programme—not as an isolated fourth practice.

05

From architecture to an operation you can govern

Begin with business-service dependencies and the estate you have. Compare platform options, engineer the selected design and validate it with representative workloads. Carry architecture decisions, security responsibilities and recovery procedures into an agreed customer-operated, co-managed or managed service.

  • A workload and dependency map, platform-options assessment and cost assumptions.
  • An implementation and transition plan covering access, migration, rollback and recovery.
  • Acceptance criteria, test findings, runbooks and a responsibility matrix for operation.
  • A review cadence for capacity, experience, protection gaps, incidents and improvement.

The scope and commercial proposal define deliverables, service hours, supported systems and response commitments. Platform certifications, staffing arrangements or vendor capabilities do not substitute for those commitments.

Connected expertise without blurred ownership

HCDW brings infrastructure, workspace delivery and recoverability into the design. Microsoft Security remains with the Microsoft practice; Enterprise Cybersecurity covers non-Microsoft security controls; Enterprise AI Engineering brings AI lifecycle and governance expertise. For Active Directory or Microsoft Entra ID recovery, the directory is a protected workload—not a transfer of Microsoft Security ownership.

Start with a resilience readiness conversation

Bring your critical services, current platforms, known recovery gaps and next infrastructure or workforce change. We can scope a readiness assessment that establishes priorities, dependencies, test needs and a phased improvement plan. Agree outputs, access, timing and commercial terms before work begins.

  • Review evidence from restore tests and operational records, not only product feature lists.
  • Use approved customer examples where relevant without assuming another organisation’s outcome applies to yours.
